Emergency preparedness · Cleveland & Bradley County

Be prepared, together.

Tornadoes, flash floods, ice storms and long power outages all hit Bradley County. Here’s how to get warned, what to keep on hand, and what to do, plus how to look out for the neighbors who’ll need help most.

3. Make a plan

Talk it through with everyone in your home, including kids. Write it down and put it on the fridge.

Your safe spot

The lowest floor, in an inside room or hallway with no windows. In a mobile home, plan where you’ll go before a warning: a sturdy building nearby or a storm-ready room.

Meet-up places

One spot right outside your home (in case of fire), and one outside your neighborhood if you can’t get home.

An out-of-town contact

Local phone lines jam in a disaster. Pick one relative or friend far away that everyone checks in with. Texts get through when calls don’t.

Medicines & power needs

Anyone who relies on oxygen, a CPAP, a power chair, insulin in the fridge or dialysis needs a backup plan for outages. Call your provider now to ask about it.

Pets

Pack food, water, leashes or a carrier, and vet records. Many shelters don’t take pets, so ask ahead or line up a friend.

Papers

Copies of IDs, insurance, prescriptions and important phone numbers in a waterproof bag, plus photos on your phone. For immigrant families: keep your rights card and emergency contacts with you.

Templates and more: Ready.gov, TEMA, and Ready.gov en español.

4. When it happens

A watch means conditions are right for it: get ready. A warning means it’s happening or about to: act now.

🌪️ Tornado

Southeast Tennessee gets tornadoes in every season, often at night and wrapped in rain where you can’t see them.

During a warning

  • Go to your safe spot right away. Get under something sturdy and cover your head and neck. Wear shoes and a helmet if you have one.
  • Leave a mobile home or car for a sturdy building. Don’t shelter under an overpass.
  • Cleveland-Bradley County opens storm-ready rooms when a severe storm is coming. Locations are announced through county alerts.

🌊 Flash flood

Creeks like the South Mouse and Candies can rise in minutes, and low roads flood fast.

  • Turn around, don’t drown. Just 12 inches of moving water can carry off a car, and 6 inches can knock you off your feet.
  • Never drive around barricades. Roads can wash out underneath.
  • Move to higher ground if water is rising. Stay out of floodwater; it can carry sewage, chemicals and live wires.

🧊 Winter storms & ice

Ice brings down trees and power lines, and outages can last days.

  • Never run a generator, grill or camp stove indoors or in a garage. Carbon monoxide kills. Keep generators 20 feet from doors and windows.
  • Don’t heat your home with the oven. Use space heaters with care and keep them 3 feet from anything that burns.
  • Drip faucets and open cabinet doors under sinks so pipes don’t freeze.
  • Need somewhere warm? Find warming centers on TEMA’s shelter map or call 211.

🥵 Extreme heat

Heat kills more people in the U.S. than any other kind of weather.

  • Drink water before you’re thirsty. Rest in the shade or A/C during the hottest hours, about 11 to 5.
  • Learn the signs: heavy sweating, dizziness or cramps mean heat exhaustion; confusion or hot, dry skin can mean heat stroke. Call 911.
  • Never leave kids or pets in a parked car, even for a minute.
  • The public library and other air-conditioned public spaces are free places to cool off.

🔦 Power outage

  • Report it: Cleveland Utilities (423) 472-4521, or check the outage maps.
  • Stay at least 35 feet from downed lines and assume they’re live.
  • Keep the fridge closed: food stays cold about 4 hours, and a full freezer about 48. When in doubt, throw it out.
  • Unplug electronics so a surge doesn’t damage them when power comes back.
  • Use flashlights, not candles.

💧 Boil-water advisory

Utilities issue these when water pressure drops or a main breaks, since germs can get into the pipes.

  • Bring water to a rolling boil for 3 minutes, then let it cool, or use bottled water. That’s Tennessee’s standard advice.
  • Use boiled or bottled water for drinking, ice, brushing teeth, baby formula, cooking and washing dishes.
  • Throw out ice, drinks and baby formula made with tap water since the advisory began.
  • Wait for your utility to say the advisory is lifted. Then run your taps for a few minutes.
  • Check notices from your water provider: Cleveland Utilities, or the district on your bill.

Look out for each other

In every disaster, the first help comes from neighbors. Before a storm, check on people who live alone, older neighbors, people without a car, families with new babies, and neighbors who are unhoused or don’t speak English. Share a ride, a charged battery, a warm room or a translated alert. That’s mutual aid.
