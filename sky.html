        This month in Cherokee · ᏣᎳᎩ

        Thirteen moons on the turtle’s back

        Many Native nations, the Cherokee among them, kept time by the moon. Teachers often explain it with a turtle shell: the 13 large plates on its back stand for the 13 moons in a year, and the 28 smaller plates around the edge for the days from one new moon to the next. Each moon had its own ceremony, set by Cherokee spiritual leaders. Later the Cherokee also gave names to the 12 months of the calendar the rest of the country used.

        Names, spellings and meanings differ between the Overhill (Tennessee) and Kituwah (North Carolina) dialects and between sources. We use Overhill forms where we can. To learn from Cherokee speakers themselves, visit the Cherokee Nation Language Department or the Eastern Band’s Kituwah Preservation & Education Program. Sources: the Cherokee calendar (citing Mooney, 1894, and Feeling’s Cherokee-English Dictionary, 1975).

            Boil-water advisory?

            Don’t drink tap water until it’s been brought to a rolling boil for 3 minutes and cooled, or use bottled water. That includes water for ice, brushing teeth, baby formula and washing dishes. Throw out ice and drinks made with the water.
